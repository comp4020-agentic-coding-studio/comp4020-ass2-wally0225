# Red Flags & Adverse Selection

Why "still single" is a market prediction, not a character read

---

## Three ideas, before anything else

- information asymmetry
- adverse selection
- unraveling

Get these three straight now — the scenario in a minute uses all of them.

---

## Information asymmetry

One side of a match can see things the other side can't — how someone
actually behaves in a relationship, versus how they present on a first
date.

Economists call this **information asymmetry**: one party knows something
relevant that the other party can't verify.

---

## Adverse selection

If you can't tell good from bad up front, and both get treated the same
way, the good ones have the most to lose from staying — so they leave
first, and who's left skews worse over time.

That's **adverse selection**. It's not a claim about anyone's character.
It's a prediction about who's still in the pool.

---

## Unraveling

Run that forward: as the average quality of "still available" drops, it
gets harder to trust *anyone* still there, so more good people leave
sooner — a market can hollow itself out this way without anyone doing
anything wrong.

Economists call this progressive exit **unraveling**.

---

## Now, back to the point

Two people. Both "still single" at 34. Same fact, different reasons.

---

## Two people, same fact

| Trait | Alex | Blair |
|---|---|---|
| Visible fact | still single at 34 | still single at 34 |
| What a stranger assumes | something's wrong with them | something's wrong with them |
| What's actually true | turned down three good relationships that weren't quite right | has been the one getting left, for reasons they haven't addressed |
| Verifiable from a profile alone | no | no |

---

## What adverse selection predicts

The market treats Alex and Blair identically — both read as a "red flag"
by the same folk logic, because the pool of people still single at 34
really does skew, on average, toward Blair's story more than Alex's.

The prediction is about the *pool*. It says nothing about which one either
of them actually is.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> The mechanism explains why the pool looks the way it does. It cannot
> tell you which person you're looking at.
