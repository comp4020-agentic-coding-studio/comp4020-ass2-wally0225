# Stable Matching

Gale–Shapley, worked example

---

## The setup

Two sides, A and B, three members each. Everyone ranks everyone on the
other side. A matching is **stable** if no pair on opposite sides would
both rather be matched to each other than to their current match.

---

## The algorithm

1. Every unmatched proposer offers to their top remaining choice
2. Each receiver holds its best offer so far, rejects the rest
3. Rejected proposers cross that name off and propose again
4. Repeat until nobody is rejected

Finite, terminates, always stable. That's the theorem.

---

## Round 1

| Proposer | Offers | Held by receiver |
|----------|--------|-------------------|
| A1 | B2 | B2 tentatively holds A1 |
| A2 | B1 | B1 tentatively holds A2 |
| A3 | B2 | B2 compares A3 to A1, keeps the better, rejects the other |

---

## Round 2

The rejected proposer crosses off the receiver that turned them down and
offers to their next-ranked choice. Their previous holder may now be
displaced in turn.

Repeat until every receiver holds exactly one proposer and nobody is
mid-rejection.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> Stable does not mean happy. It means nobody has an incentive to break
> the arrangement, given what everyone else is doing.

---

## What the theorem doesn't say

- nothing about proposer-optimal vs. receiver-optimal outcomes being equal
- nothing about the surplus once matched — that's week 9
- nothing about what happens when preferences change after matching
