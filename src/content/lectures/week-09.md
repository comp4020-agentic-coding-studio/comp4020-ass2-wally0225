The dishes were not the problem. A stable match creates a cooperative
surplus — everything the pair can produce together that neither could
alone — and the question week 8 left open is how that surplus gets split.
Nash's bargaining solution answers it under a small set of axioms
(efficiency, symmetry, invariance, independence of irrelevant alternatives)
and gives a unique split as a function of each side's outside option: what
they'd get by walking away.

This is where "market failure" stops being a metaphor for something that
happened before the match and becomes a description of what can go wrong
after it: a bad split isn't a communication problem, it's what happens when
one side's outside option quietly weakens and the other's doesn't.

## Outline

- cooperative surplus and the Nash bargaining solution
- outside options as the real variable, and why they change after matching
- why "just communicate better" mistakes a bargaining-power problem for an
  information problem
