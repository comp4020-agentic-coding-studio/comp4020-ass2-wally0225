A market is any arrangement where scarce things get allocated among people
who want them, under rules nobody fully controls. Housing is a market.
University admission is a market. This course's claim is that courtship is
one too, and that the tools economists built for the first two — search
costs, signaling, screening, stable matching, mechanism design — say
something true about the third.

The claim is not that romance is "just" economics, or that the vocabulary
of markets improves anyone's love life. It's narrower and, we think, more
interesting: wherever information is unevenly held and outcomes depend on
what other people do, markets fail in specific, nameable ways. The semester
is a tour of those failures, in order of how badly they hurt.

## Outline

- what makes something a market, and what makes a market fail
- why "love isn't a market" is the position this course exists to test, not
