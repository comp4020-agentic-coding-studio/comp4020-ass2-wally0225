Every market needs an exit option or its participants aren't really
choosing to stay — they're trapped. This week treats separation the same
way it treated formation: search costs for re-entering the market, sunk
costs that keep people past the point a fresh analysis would recommend, and
the asymmetric information problem of never quite knowing, from inside a
relationship, what your own outside option is actually worth.

Then the semester turns the model on itself. Every week has assumed markets
are the right lens; this one asks what the lens hides — the things a
market framework has no vocabulary for, and whether "failure" is even the
right word for outcomes the model simply wasn't built to see. The capstone
is where you answer that for a case of your own choosing, not for us.

## Outline

- exit as a market with its own search costs and sunk costs
- what this semester's model predicts well, and what it visibly can't
  explain
- the capstone brief, and what "stands on its own" means for a project
  built to question its own premise
